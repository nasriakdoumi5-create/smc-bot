# referrals

Read `product-marketing` first.

## Precondition

A referral program amplifies satisfaction; it cannot create it. If churn is
above ~10%/month, fix that first (`churn-prevention`) — paying people to
recruit into a leaky product accelerates the damage and burns their
reputation with their own audience.

## The mechanic that fits us

Manual fulfillment rules out most affiliate infrastructure. What works:

**Member referral — free month for free month.**
- Existing subscriber refers someone; when that person's first payment
  clears, both get a month added.
- Tracked by asking the new subscriber "who referred you?" at signup
  (`analytics` already collects the acquisition field).
- Cost is a month of a product with near-zero marginal cost. Cash rewards
  are worse: they attract spam recruiting and create a payout obligation.

**Cap it.** Maximum 3 rewarded referrals per subscriber per quarter. Without
a cap, someone will start mass-posting the link and our name ends up in spam
threads across the niche.

## Affiliate partners (separate track)

For educators and newsletter owners, a revenue share is appropriate — 30% of
the first 3 months, paid on cleared payments only. Handle these one-to-one
via `co-marketing`; do not build a public affiliate program. Public programs
in the signals niche attract exactly the promotional behavior
(`product-marketing` claim rules) that gets the whole operation labeled a
scam.

**Every affiliate gets the claim rules in writing.** An affiliate promising
guaranteed returns creates the same exposure as if we said it. Terminate on
first violation, and say so up front.

## Making referral easy

- Give subscribers something worth forwarding: the weekly recap (`emails`),
  a marked chart (`image`), a free tool (`free-tools`). People share assets,
  not links.
- Ask once, at the activation moment (`onboarding`), never repeatedly.
- Never gate product functionality behind referring. It reads as MLM
  mechanics to this audience — fatal.

## Measurement

Track: referred signups, their 90-day retention vs baseline, and reward cost.
Referred subscribers usually retain better; if they do not, the referral copy
is over-promising and `copy-edit` should tighten it.
