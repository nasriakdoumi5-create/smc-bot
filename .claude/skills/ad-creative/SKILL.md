# ad-creative

Read `product-marketing` first, then `ads` — do not produce creative for a
channel that will ban the account.

## What we can advertise

Per `ads`: the **free asset**, not the signal subscription. That constraint
shapes every hook. We are not selling signals in the ad; we are offering a
kill-zone clock, a sizing calculator, or a cheat sheet, to people who trade
NQ during specific hours.

## Hook patterns

Built from `copywriting`'s mechanism-first rule, adapted for interruption:

- `The London kill zone opens in 40 minutes. Free clock with all four sessions in your timezone.`
- `PDH, PDL, Asia high, Asia low — marked automatically. Free tool.`
- `A calculator that sizes MNQ positions against your prop-firm drawdown limit.`

Each names the instrument or the session. That self-selection is the whole
targeting strategy — the creative filters better than the ad platform does.

## Visual rules

Start from `image`'s house chart style:
- One real annotated MNQ chart, three annotations maximum.
- Instrument, timeframe, date legible.
- Text overlay ≤ 7 words, high contrast, readable at feed size.
- Watermarked.

Banned outright: money imagery, profit screenshots, account balances, luxury
signaling, stock photos of traders, red/green arrow spam, any AI-generated
"trading" imagery. Every one is a policy-review trigger *and* an ICP scam
marker — the rare case where compliance and taste agree completely.

## Compliance in creative

- No claim the destination page cannot substantiate.
- No performance figure in an ad, at all. There is no room to label
  hypothetical results properly in ad copy, so the figure does not go there.
- Risk disclosure on the landing page, prominent.

## Variant discipline

Produce 3 hooks × 2 visuals = 6 creatives maximum per test. More variants
than the budget can power to significance is waste — see `ab-testing` for the
sample-size floor.

Change one dimension at a time: same visual across hook tests, same hook
across visual tests. Otherwise the result teaches nothing.
