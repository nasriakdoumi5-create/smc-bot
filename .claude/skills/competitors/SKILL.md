# competitors

Read `product-marketing` first.

## Who we are actually compared against

The buyer is not choosing between signal services. They are choosing between
*ways to solve the same problem*:

1. **Other signal channels** (Telegram/Discord, $20–100/mo). Mostly opaque,
   heavily promotional, frequently deleted history. Our differentiator is
   transparency — published losses and an unedited archive.
2. **Paid TradingView indicators** ($15–60/mo or lifetime). Direct
   competition for the indicator SKUs. They usually offer no alert delivery.
3. **Prop-firm-adjacent tools** (journaling, risk). Adjacent, mostly
   partners rather than rivals (`co-marketing`).
4. **DIY** — the trader marks their own levels. The largest competitor by
   share, and the honest comparison is time saved and rules enforced
   mechanically.
5. **Doing nothing.** Also large. Beaten by making the free surface useful
   enough to build a habit (`free-tools`, `community`).

## How to run the analysis

For each rival, record only what changes a decision:

- Price and what is actually included.
- What they claim (screenshot it — claims change when challenged).
- What proof they show and whether losses appear anywhere.
- Delivery mechanism: alerts, chart only, or manual posts.
- Instrument and session coverage.
- Where their audience came from.

Keep it in one table, updated quarterly. A 20-page competitive deck is waste
at this scale.

## The gaps we can occupy

From this landscape, three positions are genuinely open:

- **Unedited history.** Almost nobody publishes losses. It is free to do and
  structurally hard for a hype-marketed competitor to copy — doing so
  contradicts their own claims.
- **Session discipline as a feature.** Most services maximize signal count
  because volume looks like value. Refusing to fire is differentiated.
- **Visible plumbing.** Publishing the alert payload and webhook design
  attracts technical buyers that opaque services cannot serve.

## Rules

- Never name a competitor in public marketing. It elevates them, and in this
  niche it invites retaliation and drama that costs more than the comparison
  gains.
- Never repeat a competitor's claim as fact, even to rebut it.
- Comparison content stays category-level: "most channels don't publish
  losses" is fine; naming one is not.
