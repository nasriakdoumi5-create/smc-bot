# youtube-channel-plan

Read `product-marketing` first. This skill is the YouTube branch's root — the
other six (`youtube-niche`, `youtube-scripts`, `youtube-algorithm`,
`youtube-production`, `youtube-monetization`, `youtube-analytics`) each go
deep on one part of what this file assembles.

## What the channel is actually for

Decide this before anything else, because it changes every downstream choice:

**YouTube is the top of the funnel for a $45/mo product, not an AdSense
business.** At our scale, ad revenue on a trading channel might reach
$3–8 per 1,000 views. One subscriber to the full package is worth more than
10,000 views. So the channel optimizes for *qualified* viewers who become
subscribers — never for raw view count.

This single decision resolves most arguments later: it is why we do not chase
broad "day trading" content, why the description CTA matters more than watch
time, and why a 2,000-view video that converts four subscribers beats a
50,000-view video that converts none.

## Channel positioning

Same as the product (`product-marketing`), stated for video:

> Session-gated SMC on NQ/MNQ, shown on the chart, with the losses included.

The differentiator on YouTube specifically is **unedited outcomes**. The
niche is saturated with channels showing only wins. A channel that publishes
every flagged setup — the stopped-out ones too — is doing something
structurally difficult for a hype channel to copy (`marketing-psychology`).

## Content pillars

Four, mapped to the product and to `content`'s article pillars so video and
article reinforce each other:

| Pillar | Share | Job | Product it routes to |
|---|---|---|---|
| Session recaps | 40% | Proof | Signals channel |
| Mechanism explainers | 30% | Search traffic, trust | Both indicators |
| Automation / build logs | 20% | Technical buyers | BaaS, indicators |
| Data studies | 10% | Differentiation, links | Everything |

## Formats and cadence

Be honest about one operator whose filming window is constrained by the
sessions themselves (07:00–11:00 and 13:30–17:00 UTC):

- **1 long-form per week** (8–15 min). The session recap or a mechanism piece.
- **3 Shorts per week**, cut from the long-form (`youtube-production`).
- That is it. A cadence you can hold for 12 months beats a burst of daily
  uploads followed by silence — the algorithm punishes the silence more than
  it rewarded the burst (`youtube-algorithm`).

Publish at a fixed day and time, after the NY session closes so the recap is
same-day.

## Growth systems

1. **Search-first for half the library.** Mechanism explainers target real
   queries (`seo-audit` has the keyword list — the same terms work on
   YouTube search).
2. **Browse/suggested for the recaps.** These win on thumbnail and title
   (`youtube-scripts`), not on search.
3. **Shorts as discovery**, long-form as conversion. Shorts rarely convert
   directly; they build the subscriber base that watches the recaps.
4. **Cross-post** to X and the Telegram channel (`social`, `community`) —
   our existing audience is the first 100 subscribers, and early engagement
   is disproportionately valuable.

## Retention framework

Retention is where trading channels fail: they open with a greeting and a
logo animation. The house rule is the chart is on screen within 5 seconds.
Full technique list in `youtube-scripts`.

## Monetization staging

Detailed in `youtube-monetization`. In short: the product CTA exists from
video one; YPP is a milestone, not a strategy; affiliate links are mostly
declined because prop-firm affiliate deals compromise the independence that
makes the channel credible.

## The weekly schedule

| Day | Block | Work |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | 1h | Review last week's analytics (`youtube-analytics`), pick the topic |
| Tue | 2h | Script + chart prep (`youtube-scripts`) |
| Wed | 2h | Record + edit |
| Thu | 1h | Thumbnail, title, description, tags; schedule |
| Fri | 1h | Publish after NY close; cut 3 Shorts; cross-post |
| Daily | 15m | Reply to every comment for the first 48h |

Roughly 7 hours a week. If that is not available, drop to biweekly long-form
rather than lowering quality — `youtube-algorithm` explains why inconsistent
quality hurts more than lower frequency.

## Compliance on YouTube specifically

Everything in `product-marketing`'s claim rules applies, plus:
- No thumbnail showing a P&L figure or account balance.
- No title promising returns ("How I made $10k") — it is both a claim
  violation and a magnet for the wrong audience.
- Affiliate or sponsor relationships disclosed in-video and in the
  description; the platform requires it and so does the FTC.
- The risk disclosure goes in every description and on screen at the end.
