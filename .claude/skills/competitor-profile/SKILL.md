# competitor-profile

Read `product-marketing` first, then `competitors` for the landscape context.

## When to build one

Only when a specific rival is affecting decisions: named repeatedly by
prospects (`sales-enablement`), cited in cancellations
(`churn-prevention`), or dominating a keyword we need (`seo-audit`).
Otherwise the landscape table in `competitors` is enough.

## Profile template

**Identity** — name, operator, how long active, where based, team size signal.

**Offer** — exact SKUs, prices, billing terms, what is included, trial policy,
refund policy.

**Funnel** — trace it yourself: where their traffic comes from, the landing
page, the signup flow, the fulfillment mechanics, the onboarding messages.
Sign up for their free tier and document it. This is the most useful hour of
the whole exercise.

**Claims** — verbatim, screenshotted, dated. Note specifically: do they
publish losses? Is history editable or deleted? Are results labeled
hypothetical? Their claim posture tells you how they will behave when
challenged, and where they are fragile.

**Product** — instruments, sessions, timeframes, delivery mechanism, alert
content, whether a chart tool is included.

**Distribution** — the channels that actually produce for them: paid (and
how they clear policy), organic, affiliates, partnerships.

**Social proof** — reviews, community size, engagement rate vs follower count.
A 40k-member channel with 30 views per post is bought.

**Weaknesses** — where a buyer would be underserved.

## Turning it into action

End every profile with three lines, or it was not worth writing:

1. What they do better that we should copy.
2. What they do worse that we should make visible (category-level, unnamed,
   per `competitors`).
3. One concrete change to our positioning, copy, or product, with an owner.

## Ethics

Research what any buyer could see. Do not misrepresent yourself to obtain
private material, do not scrape their subscriber lists, do not redistribute
their paid content, and do not buy access under a false identity. In a small
niche this is discovered, and the reputational cost dwarfs the intelligence.
