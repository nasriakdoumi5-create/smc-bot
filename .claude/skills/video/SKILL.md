# video

Read `product-marketing` first.

## Why video matters here

Screen-recorded proof is the hardest asset to fake and the easiest for a
skeptical trader to verify. A three-minute recording of the chart, the alert
arriving, and the trade playing out does more than a page of copy.

## The three formats

**1. Session recap (weekly, 5–8 min)**
Screen record the MNQ 5m chart over the session. Walk through each flagged
setup: the level swept, the kill-zone timing, the rating, the outcome.
Include the losers. This is the flagship format — it is proof, education, and
product demo simultaneously.

**2. Mechanism explainer (3–5 min)**
One concept: what a PDH sweep is, why the Asia range matters, why VWAP bands
use ATR. No product mention until the final 15 seconds, and then only as
"this is automated in X".

**3. Product walkthrough (90 sec)**
Alert fires in Telegram → the payload fields → the same setup on the chart →
the outcome. Show the actual JSON. Technical buyers trust visible plumbing.

## Script structure

- **0–5s**: the claim or the question, stated flat. No intro animation, no
  "hey guys welcome back". The audience leaves during a greeting.
- **5–20s**: the chart is on screen and something is being marked.
- **Body**: one point per chart move. Narrate what is being clicked.
- **Last 15s**: one CTA, spoken and on-screen, matching `copywriting`.

## Production rules

- Real screen recordings, never mockups or motion graphics of fake charts.
- Full instrument + timeframe + date visible in frame at all times.
- Risk disclosure on screen at the end and in the description.
- Captions burned in — most views are muted, and much of the Arabic-speaking
  audience reads English better than it hears it.
- Thumbnail: the chart with the marked level, large text, legible at 120px.
  See `image`.
- Publish the raw recording even when the week was bad. Selective publishing
  is detectable and it is the fastest way to lose this audience.
