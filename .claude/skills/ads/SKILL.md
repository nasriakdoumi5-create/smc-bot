# ads

Read `product-marketing` first, including the note that paid is mostly closed
to us. This skill's first job is gatekeeping, not campaign building.

## Platform reality for trading signals

| Platform | Status | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Meta (FB/IG) | Effectively closed | Financial products policy; "signals" copy triggers account bans, often permanent and tied to the business manager |
| Google Search | Restricted | Financial services verification required in many regions; CFD/signals ads frequently disapproved |
| YouTube | Same as Google | Plus stricter creative review |
| X/Twitter | Possible | Weaker enforcement, and the ICP is actually there |
| Reddit | Possible | Subreddit targeting works; hostile audience to promotion |
| Newsletter sponsorships | **Best option** | No platform policy layer, pre-qualified audience |
| TradingView profile visibility | Free adjacent | See `aso` |

**Default recommendation: do not run Meta or Google ads for the signals
offer.** A ban costs the asset permanently. Sponsorships and organic
(`social`, `content`, `community`) carry the load.

## If running paid anyway

- Advertise the **free asset**, never the paid signal offer. Send traffic to
  a `free-tools` page or a `lead-magnets` download. This clears most policy
  review and builds a list we own.
- Landing page must carry full risk disclosure and no performance claims —
  reviewers read the destination, not just the ad.
- Start at a budget you can lose entirely. Our margin math
  (`pricing`, `revops`) means a $45/mo subscriber with manual fulfillment
  supports a low CAC ceiling — model it before spending.
- Never scale before `analytics` can attribute a *paid* subscriber, not a
  click.

## Newsletter sponsorship playbook

The channel that actually fits us:
1. Find futures/prop-trading newsletters with 2k–30k subscribers
   (`prospecting`).
2. Buy one insertion, not a package.
3. Use a dedicated landing page and a unique UTM (`analytics`).
4. Judge on subscribers at day 30, not clicks.

## Kill rules

Stop a campaign when: CAC exceeds 3 months of subscription value, or
fulfillment backlog (`revops`) exceeds the promised SLA, or any platform
issues a policy warning — the second warning is usually the ban.
