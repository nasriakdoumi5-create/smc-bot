# youtube-niche

Read `product-marketing` first, then `youtube-channel-plan`.

## Our niche is already chosen

The product decides it: **SMC/ICT execution on NQ/MNQ index futures, session-
gated.** Do not re-open this. What genuinely remains open are two decisions
that matter more than most people's niche choice:

### Decision 1: language

| | English | Arabic |
|---|---|---|
| Audience size | Very large | Much smaller |
| Competition | Saturated; established channels with editors | Thin, especially for futures (most Arabic trading content is forex/crypto) |
| RPM | Higher | Lower |
| Our fit | We have the content | We have native fluency and an existing audience |

**Recommendation: Arabic-first, English second.** The English futures-SMC
lane is contested by full-time creators with production teams; we would be
the thousandth such channel. The Arabic futures lane is close to empty, our
existing Telegram audience is there, and the product is already bilingual.
Start Arabic, and republish the highest-performing pieces in English once the
format is proven. This inverts the usual advice deliberately — a small
uncontested pond beats an ocean where we cannot be found.

### Decision 2: face or no face

Screen-recorded charts with voiceover is sufficient and much faster
(`youtube-production`). A face builds trust faster in a niche full of
anonymous scammers, but it is not required if the outcomes are unedited.
Recommendation: voice and charts, no face, with the real name in the
description. Reassess at 1,000 subscribers.

## Validating any lane

The six-factor check, applied to ours:

1. **Audience demand** — real. Prop-firm accounts have exploded; NQ is the
   most-traded index future among retail day traders.
2. **Competition** — high in English, low in Arabic. See above.
3. **Search potential** — strong for mechanism queries (`kill zone times`,
   `liquidity sweep`, `PDH PDL`), weak for recaps (browse-driven instead).
4. **Monetization** — strong, but through the product, not AdSense
   (`youtube-monetization`).
5. **Content longevity** — high. Kill-zone mechanics do not expire; a good
   explainer earns views for years. Recaps are the opposite: disposable by
   design, which is why they are 40% and not 90%.
6. **Differentiation** — publishing losses, session discipline, and visible
   plumbing (`competitors`).

## The target viewer

One person, specific enough to write for:

> A trader with a $50k funded evaluation account, 1–3 years in, trading MNQ
> on the 5m during the New York session. They have a strategy but break it
> under pressure. Their nightmare is a rule breach, not a missed winner. They
> already watch two or three English channels and are skeptical of all of
> them. They are looking for a system that says no more often than it says
> yes.

Every title gets tested against that person. If they would not click it, it
does not get made.

## Content categories

The four pillars from `youtube-channel-plan`, each with its format:

- **Session recaps** — screen recording, 8–12 min, published after NY close.
- **Mechanism explainers** — 6–10 min, evergreen, search-targeted.
- **Automation / build logs** — 10–15 min, code and webhooks on screen.
- **Data studies** — 8–12 min, backtest output from this repo's engines.

## The idea bank

100 ranked video ideas are in `references/video-ideas.md`, grouped by pillar
and ordered by growth potential within each group. Take the top of the list
first — they are ranked by search demand against production cost, not by what
is most interesting to make.

To extend the list: every question asked in the Telegram channel
(`community`) is a video, and every article in `content` has a video twin.
